Google AI Overviews for Law Firms: Why Your Firm Is Missing From the Answer

When an injured person in your city searches Google for a personal injury lawyer, they may never reach the list of blue links. Above the results, an AI Overview writes the answer for them, and it sometimes names a few firms by name. If yours is not one of them, the case is often decided before your website ever loads.

Most managing partners still measure success by where they rank on Google. That ruler no longer captures what is happening at the top of the page. Google now places a generated summary above the organic results on a large and growing share of searches. The prospect reads the summary, absorbs the names inside it, and frequently stops there. The ten blue links you spent years climbing are now below the fold, below the answer, and below the moment the decision gets made.

For a personal injury firm spending five to twenty thousand dollars a month on marketing, this is not a small shift. It is a new gatekeeper sitting between your firm and every prospect who starts on Google.

What a Google AI Overview Actually Is

An AI Overview is the boxed summary Google generates at the top of a search result, drawn from across the web and written in a few confident sentences. For an informational query it explains a concept. For a query with commercial intent, such as someone looking for a lawyer, it can summarize what to look for and point to specific firms and sources.

The important part is placement. The overview sits above everything else. Independent trackers across the SEO industry in 2026 have put AI Overview presence anywhere from roughly a quarter of all Google searches to nearly half, depending on the query set and how each study measures it, and the coverage has climbed steadily year over year. Whatever the exact figure, the direction is not in dispute. The answer is moving up, and the links are moving down.

Ranking first used to mean being seen first. Now the AI Overview is seen first, and your ranking is what the reader scrolls past on the way to something they may never reach.

Why This Hits Personal Injury Firms Especially Hard

Two things make legal search unusually exposed to this change. The first is intent. A person looking for a personal injury lawyer is anxious, often in pain, and wants to be told what to do. A summary that says here is what matters and here are firms that fit is exactly the reassurance they are looking for. They lean on it.

The second is money. Because legal cases are valuable, the marketing around them is expensive and crowded, which means the difference between being named in the answer and being absent from it is measured in signed cases, not in traffic. A single motor vehicle case can be worth more than a year of ad spend. Losing your place in the answer is not a vanity metric. It is lost intake.

The Click You Used to Win Now Happens Less Often

The overview does not only reorder the page. It absorbs the click. When the answer is already on the screen, fewer people travel to any website at all. Ahrefs, studying a large sample of queries, found that when an AI Overview is present, clicks to the top organic result fall by roughly a third. SparkToro's 2026 analysis of Google search behavior found that fewer than one in three searches now ends in a click to the open web at all.

Read those two findings together and the picture is stark. More of the answer is being resolved on Google's own page, and of the shrinking number of people who do click through, they click on whatever the overview surfaced first. If your firm is not inside that summary, you are competing for a slice of attention that is smaller every quarter.

Why Google's AI Names One Firm and Not Another

An AI Overview is not pulling names out of thin air, and it is not simply reading the current rankings. It assembles an answer from what it can confidently understand about the entities involved. For a law firm, that understanding is built from a handful of signals that either line up or do not.

The first is entity clarity. Google has to be sure which firm you are. The same name, address, and practice areas repeated identically across your site, your directory listings, and third party sources tell it you are a single, real, consistent entity. Conflicting or thin information makes you a firm it would rather not vouch for out loud.

The second is structured data. Schema markup for a legal service, an attorney, and reviews spells out for Google what you do, where you do it, and who leads the work. Many firm sites were built before this markup mattered and still run without it. That absence forces the model to guess, and it tends not to name the firms it has to guess about.

The third is geographic specificity. An overview answering a query about a named city favors firms whose content ties them clearly and repeatedly to that city and county. Copy that says we serve clients statewide reads as vague. Copy that names the metro, the courts, and the roads where cases happen reads as local and specific, and specificity is what a location query rewards.

The fourth is third party confirmation. Google trusts a firm more when independent sources agree it matters. Bar association pages, established legal directories, reputable local coverage, and consistent review profiles all act as outside votes. A firm with a large website but no external footprint has told Google only its own side of the story.

How to Check Whether You Appear in AI Overviews Right Now

You can run a rough test in a few minutes. On a browser where you are not signed into your own firm's Google account, search the queries a real prospect would use. Try best personal injury lawyer in your city. Try who to call after a car accident in your county. Try top rated injury attorney near me from a phone in your market.

Watch what forms at the very top. Does an AI Overview appear. Does it name firms. Is yours among them, or is it a set of competitors while you sit in the links underneath. Run each query a few times, because the overview varies, and note the pattern rather than a single result.

That test will tell you whether you have a problem. What it will not tell you is why the named firms are being chosen, which of your signals are weak, and which fix would move you first. Answering that means looking at the sources behind the overview and comparing your entity, schema, and citation footprint against the firms winning the slot. That comparison is the work.

What Getting Named Actually Takes

Earning a place in an AI Overview is not a rewrite of your website. It is a focused effort on the signals the model reads, and the work falls into three parts.

Close the structured data gaps first. Add or correct legal service, attorney, and review schema across the site so Google can categorize you without guessing. For most firms this is a few hours of developer time and it produces lasting signal.

Build the outside footprint next. Make sure your firm appears with identical information across the major legal directories, that your attorneys show up in association databases and credible local coverage, and that your review profiles are consistent. Every one of those is an outside vote Google can check against your own pages.

Then make your content local and specific. Write practice area and location pages that name the cities, counties, and case types you actually handle, in the plain language a prospect would use when they ask. Specific, verifiable, locally grounded content is what a location query rewards.

Done in that order, the work compounds. Each improvement to your entity clarity, structured data, footprint, and content raises the odds that the next overview in your market speaks your name instead of a competitor's.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Google AI Overview for a law firm?

A Google AI Overview is the generated summary that can appear at the top of a Google search, above the organic links. For a legal query it may explain what to look for in a lawyer and name specific firms. If your firm is named, prospects see it before any link. Why is my firm not showing up in Google AI Overviews?

Usually because the signals Google relies on to name a firm are weak or inconsistent. Common causes are missing legal schema markup, an inconsistent business name and address across the web, vague geographic content that does not tie you clearly to your city, and a thin footprint on outside directories and review sites. The overview tends to name firms it can verify with confidence.

Are Google AI Overviews different from ChatGPT recommendations?

They are separate surfaces but they reward similar things. An AI Overview appears inside Google search, while ChatGPT, Claude, and Perplexity answer inside their own apps. All of them decide which firm to name based on how clearly they understand your firm and how much independent evidence supports it, so improving one usually helps the others.

Do AI Overviews really reduce clicks to law firm websites?

Yes. Independent research in 2026 found that when an AI Overview is present, clicks to the top organic result drop sharply, and the majority of Google searches now end without a click to any website. More of the decision happens on Google's own page, which is why being named inside the overview matters more than ranking below it.
