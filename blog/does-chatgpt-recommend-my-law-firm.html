Does ChatGPT Recommend My Law Firm? How to Check in 15 Minutes

By LogicRove. Published November 2026.

If you have never asked ChatGPT for a personal injury lawyer in your own city, you do not know whether your firm is being recommended. Your prospects are asking. This is how to run the same test they run, read the result honestly, and decide what to do next.

You do not need software for a first look. You need a clean browser, fifteen minutes, and a willingness to see a result you may not like. The goal is a plain answer to one question: when a hurt person asks an AI assistant who to call, does it say your name?

Step One: Write the Questions Your Client Actually Types

Skip your firm name and your marketing language. Write the questions a frightened, injured person would type. Examples include "who is the best car accident lawyer in Dallas," "what personal injury attorney should I call after a truck crash in Atlanta," and "top rated personal injury firm near me." Write ten of them. Mix practice areas, such as car accidents, truck accidents, slip and fall, and wrongful death, and attach your metro to each one.

Step Two: Run Each Question Across Several Assistants

Ask each question in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Claude, and search the same phrasing on Google to see whether an AI Overview appears. Use a signed out or private window where you can, so your own history does not shape the answer. Different assistants pull from different sources, so a firm named in one can be missing in another. Record every result.

Step Three: Repeat Each Question at Least Three Times

AI answers are not fixed. The same question asked twice can return different firms. One lucky mention proves little, and one miss proves little. Ask each question several times and note how often your firm appears. A firm named in most runs has a real position. A firm named once in a while has a weak one. A firm never named has a gap to close.

Step Four: Keep a Simple Scorecard

Create a table with one row per question and one column per assistant. In each cell, write how many times your firm was named out of the runs you did, and list the competitors that were named instead. After an hour of work you will have a real map. Pay attention to patterns. If one competitor appears in nearly every answer, that firm is doing something you are not, and you can study it.

Step Five: Read the Sources the Assistant Cites

Perplexity and Google AI Overviews usually show where an answer came from. Open those links. You will often find the same small group of directories, legal publications, and review sites behind the recommendations. If those sources list your competitors and not you, you have found a specific, fixable reason for your absence. This is the most useful part of the test, and most firms skip it.

What a Poor Result Usually Means

A firm that does not appear is rarely a worse firm. It is usually a firm whose signals are harder for a machine to read. The common causes are missing or incomplete structured data, anonymous pages with no named attorney, inconsistent name and phone details across directories, thin presence on the sources AI systems trust, and content written to persuade rather than to answer. Each of these can be repaired, and they are easier to fix in that order than most firms expect.

What a Good Result Still Needs to Be Checked For

If your firm is named, do not stop. Check whether the description is accurate. Assistants sometimes state the wrong practice areas, an old address, or a former partner. A recommendation that sends a prospect to a dead number does not help you. Correct the underlying sources so the accurate version wins out.

The Limits of a Manual Test

A fifteen minute check shows you the shape of the problem. It does not give you statistical confidence, and it does not tell you which of several possible fixes will move the result most. Answers vary by city, by wording, and by day, so a handful of runs can mislead in either direction. For a decision you will spend real budget on, you want a larger, documented sample across more assistants, with the competitors named and the cited sources recorded.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I check if ChatGPT recommends my law firm?
Write ten questions a prospect would ask, such as who the best personal injury lawyer in your city is. Ask each one in ChatGPT several times, record how often your firm is named and which competitors appear, then repeat the process in Perplexity, Gemini, and Claude.
Why does ChatGPT give different law firms each time I ask?
AI assistants generate answers rather than retrieve a fixed list, so wording, timing, and the sources available can change the result. That is why you should ask each question multiple times and track how often your firm appears.
Is my law firm invisible to AI if Google ranks it well?
Not necessarily, but it is possible. Google rankings and AI recommendations draw on overlapping but different signals, including structured data, authorship, and third party sources. A firm can rank well on Google and still be skipped by AI assistants.
What is the fastest fix if ChatGPT never names my firm?
Align your firm's name, address, phone number, and practice areas across your website and directory listings, then repair your schema markup. These steps remove the most common reasons an assistant lacks confidence in naming a firm.
